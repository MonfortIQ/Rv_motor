The Complete Step-by-Step RV Winterization & Freeze Guard Guide

Carlos Morales (HVAC & Plumbing Lead) September 28, 2026 8 min read

Winterizing an RV is the single most critical preventive maintenance procedure of the year. If done incorrectly, expanding ice will crack toilet foot valves, shatter PEX manifolds, and destroy $1,200 water heaters before spring arrives.

In this guide, our certified plumbing team breaks down the two industry-standard methods: compressed air blowout and propylene glycol pink antifreeze induction—and why combining both yields foolproof freeze defense.

Step 1: Drain Fresh Water Tank & Low-Point Drains

Open your fresh water tank drain valve and uncapped hot/cold low-point drains beneath the coach. Open all faucets inside to break the vacuum and allow gravity to evacuate the bulk water.

Step 2: Bypass & Drain the Water Heater

Crucial Step: Never pump antifreeze directly into your 6 or 10-gallon water heater tank. It wastes valuable gallons of antifreeze and can corrode the electric heating element.

Turn off the water heater breaker. Locate the bypass valves behind the heater cabinet and switch the 3-valve manifold into bypass mode. Then remove the exterior nylon drain plug or sacrificial anode rod with a 1-1/16" socket to completely drain the tank.

Step 3: Compressed Air Blowout (Max 30 PSI)

Connect a brass blowout plug to your city water inlet. Set an oil-free air compressor regulator to no more than 30 PSI. Blow compressed air through each faucet fixture one at a time—starting with the highest sink and ending with the toilet and exterior shower.

Step 4: Non-Toxic Propylene Glycol Antifreeze Induction

Using the onboard water pump winterization bypass hose, draw virgin -50°F RV antifreeze directly from the gallon jug. Cycle each faucet until solid pink liquid streams from the tap. Ensure you flush the toilet until pink pools in the bowl to protect the fragile foot pedal valve.
