Essential RV Maintenance Checklist Before a Long-Distance Trip

Arthur Henderson (Master RVIA Certified Tech) October 12, 2026 6 min read

Hitting the open highway in a motorhome or towing a travel trailer represents the pinnacle of travel freedom. However, that freedom relies on dozens of mechanical, electrical, and plumbing sub-systems functioning under relentless highway vibration.

At RoadNest, more than 40% of emergency roadside repairs we encounter could have been completely prevented by a thorough 45-minute pre-trip walkaround. Before setting your GPS for the national parks or cross-country highways, follow this comprehensive maintenance checklist.

1. Running Gear: Cold Tire Pressures & Lug Nut Torque

Tire blowouts are the single most dangerous catastrophic failure on recreational vehicles. Unlike passenger vehicles that often ride on under-inflated tires with minor fuel penalty, an under-inflated RV tire overheats internally within 40 miles at highway speeds, leading to tread separation that can rip away aluminum wheel wells and sever wiring harnesses.

  • Check cold inflation pressures: Always measure pressure in the morning before sunlight hits the tire sidewall or before the coach has rolled more than one mile. Match the PSI indicated on the federal vehicle certification tag, not the maximum PSI stamped on the tire.
  • Inspect DOT date stamps: Even with pristine tread depth, RV tires degrade chemically after 5 to 6 years of UV and ozone exposure. Check the 4-digit code (e.g., "2421" means 24th week of 2021).
  • Torque trailer lug nuts: Wheel studs on trailers flex significantly. Use a calibrated torque wrench set to manufacturer specs (typically 90 to 120 ft-lbs for 1/2" studs) in a star pattern.

2. 12V Auxiliary House Battery Health

Your RV’s house batteries power everything from interior LED lighting and water pumps to slide-out room actuators and refrigerator circuit boards. If your battery bank has dropped below 12.0 volts (50% state of charge for AGM/lead-acid), the sudden surge of deploying your leveling jacks or running the furnace fan will trigger low-voltage inverter faults.

Ensure battery terminals are clean and torqued. If you run wet lead-acid batteries, inspect electrolyte fluid levels and replenish with distilled water only.

3. Propane LP System Leak Check

Before turning your tank service valves on, perform a visual inspection of your two-stage propane regulator. Check the vent hole for mud dauber nests or corrosion. Spray soapy water across copper flare fittings and rubber pigtails to verify zero micro-bubbles before igniting your water heater or furnace.

RoadNest Pro-Tip

Always test your 12V carbon monoxide and LP gas detectors inside the living quarters. These safety alarms expire every 5 years and must be replaced even if the battery light is green.

4. Rooftop Seams & Slide-Out Wiper Seals

Climb your rear ladder and inspect all Dicor self-leveling lap sealant seams around roof vents, front caps, and skylights. Highway wind speeds push rainwater against transitions at 70 MPH. Any hairline void will force moisture into the ceiling insulation.
